Why NAMs?

Conventional speech production relies on complex physiological mechanisms where air expelled from the lungs vibrates the vocal folds, while the tongue, lips, and cheeks shape this air flow into articulate sounds. This process breaks down in cases of vocal tract pathology. Beyond clinical scenarios, daily situations also demand alternatives to conventional speech. These constraints have motivated research into Silent Speech Interfaces (SSI), which aim to capture non-acoustic physiological signals associated with speech production and decode them into intelligible speech. Among SSI approaches, Non-Audible Murmur (NAM) microphones offer a compelling alternative by capturing tissue-conducted vibrations through a contact sensor placed behind the ear during silent articulation. Unlike other methods, NAM requires no invasive attachments, bulky imaging equipment, or controlled lighting, enabling a software-centric pathway for NAM-to-speech conversion.

Stage 2: Speaker-modulated Generations

Once we have the universal NAMs, we use a small diffusion transformer conditioned on speaker features to generate NAMs that follow speaker-specific features such as spectral flatness, pitch and temporal jitter.

Model Architecture

Encoder-Decoder Flow

PISA utilizes a sophisticated transformer-based encoder-decoder architecture specifically optimized for physiological signal processing. The encoder captures subtle tissue-conducted vibrations, while the decoder synthesizes high-fidelity mel-spectrograms through an iterative denoising process.

Cross-Modal Attention

To ensure alignment between NAM vibrations and linguistic content, PISA incorporates cross-modal attention blocks. These blocks dynamically weight features from both phonetic priors and acoustic-tissue signals, ensuring intelligible and natural speech reconstruction even in challenging capture conditions.

Training Paradigm

Objective Functions

Our optimization framework combines multi-scale spectral convergence loss with adversarial penalties. This dual-objective approach ensures that the model preserves global acoustic structures while refining fine-grained temporal details of the reconstructed speech.

Strategy: Curriculum Denoising

Training follows a curriculum learning strategy, gradually increasing the noise levels and linguistic complexity. This ensures stable convergence and allows the diffusion model to learn robust mappings from degraded NAM inputs to clean acoustic space across various speaker profiles.
